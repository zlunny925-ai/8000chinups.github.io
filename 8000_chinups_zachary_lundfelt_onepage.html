Rep standard

Strict. Clear. Countable.

What counts as a rep:

  • Supinated chin‑ups on a straight bar.
  • Full lockout / near dead‑hang at the bottom.
  • Chin clearly over the bar at the top.
  • No kipping, no bouncing, no lower‑body momentum.

The goal is unambiguous judging and repeatable training specificity.

Environment:

  • Standard gym straight bar (no rotating grips).
  • Indoor, climate‑controlled if possible.
  • Chalk allowed; tape only for skin protection (tears), no straps.

Consistency protects elbows, grip, and skin—and keeps verification clean.

The math

Reality check → pacing strategy.

Average

8,000 / 24 hours = 333 reps/hour

That’s ~5–6 reps/min sustained, before breaks.

Execution

Micro‑sets + short breaks

Small sets spread across each hour, plus planned resets.

Failure modes

Hands + elbows + hydration

Skin tears and tendon pain end attempts faster than "tired lats".

Verification

Trust is earned on camera + on paper.

Default verification plan:

  • Continuous‑ish video recording with a clear angle showing ROM.
  • Two‑person count log (rotating verifiers).
  • Hourly totals recorded and cross‑checked.

This is the baseline standard even if a formal record body is pursued in parallel.

What you’ll be able to see:

  • Rep standard explained clearly before the attempt.
  • Counting method visible (clicker + written logs).
  • Post‑attempt recap: totals, timestamps, and verification artifacts.

Safety protocol

Record attempt ends if safety goes sideways.
Hard stop criteria — stop immediately and seek medical evaluation
Dark/cola urine • severe swelling • profound weakness • severe pain with systemic symptoms • confusion • inability to urinate • dehydration symptoms not improving with fluids + sodium.

Hydration & electrolytes (long sessions / attempt):

  • 500–750 mL/hour fluids.
  • 300–600 mg sodium/hour minimum (up to ~1,000 mg/hour in heavy sweat/heat).

Hydration without sodium is a trap. Sodium without fluids is also a trap. Balance wins.

Medication / stimulant rule:

  • Avoid NSAIDs during extreme efforts.
  • Caffeine is strategic (no “panic dosing”).
  • Nothing new on event day—every strategy is tested in simulations first.

Kidney risk management is non‑negotiable.
